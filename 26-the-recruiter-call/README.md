# 26 · The recruiter call: why Jane Street, the money, notice, and the other screening questions, word for word

[← The application](../25-the-application/README.md) · [Index](../README.md)

Jane Street's recruiter call comes before or around the phone screen ([block 01](../01-process/README.md)). The person on it isn't an engineer. They're checking that you can explain yourself plainly, that your reason for Jane Street is specific, that you know what you want, that you can actually start, and that the team will want to talk to you. Every answer below is written to be said aloud, timed, with nothing a recruiter would need to translate. Square brackets are yours to fill before the call. [Block 13](../13-behavioural/README.md) has the interview versions of the same questions. Tags: **[JS]** reported in a Jane Street loop or stated by Jane Street, **[curated]** asked on most recruiter calls, **[practice]** written for rehearsal.

---

## Before the call

1. **Your number, decided** with the worksheet [below](#your-number-the-worksheet), even though at Jane Street you'll try to let them go first.
2. **Notice period from the contract,** plus any garden-leave, non-compete or non-solicit clause. Read them; don't remember them.
3. **What you'd leave behind:** unvested deferred awards and their vest dates.
4. **Where your other processes stand,** with real dates.
5. **One sentence on what's changed since you last applied** ([block 25](../25-the-application/README.md)).
6. **Three questions to ask**, from the list [below](#questions-to-ask-them).

## The answers

<details>
<summary><b>Walk me through your background.</b> [curated]</summary>

60 seconds, builder first, no bank vocabulary:

> I'm a security engineer who builds. At J.P. Morgan I own product security for a new cross-asset trading platform: how identity, access and secrets work for a platform other engineering teams build on. I also run the bank's AI security programme across EMEA, so securing AI agents is my day job. Outside the bank I founded Threat-modeling.io, a Python product I wrote and run, and I've built a multi-agent tool that verifies security findings by trying them in a sandbox. Before that I grew the security team at Wise from five to thirteen, ran security at Lockwood Publishing, and did offensive security consulting for capital-markets clients at Synopsys. What ties it together is that I like building the tool, not just writing the policy.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Why Jane Street?</b> [JS, asked in nearly every loop]</summary>

**30 seconds:**

> Three reasons. The security team describes itself as programmers and security experts, and that's how I work: I'd rather build the tool than buy it. Second, the culture: teaching, admitting what you don't know, and very little weight on seniority, which suits me. Third, the problem. Jane Street runs AI agents on developer machines with allow-list sandboxes and a separate identity for the AI, and securing agents is exactly what I spend my time on. I'd like to work on that with people who build their own infrastructure.

**90 seconds**, if they say "tell me more":

> The first reason is the team. Jane Street's own description of cybersecurity is a group of programmers and security experts who cover for each other across software engineering, operations and risk. That's the shape of work I'm best at, and it's rare. In most places security is a function that reviews other people's work. Here it builds.
>
> The second is how people work. Jane Street builds almost everything in-house, it teaches constantly, and it talks openly about mistakes. I've trained more than 450 engineers at the bank; I'd rather be in a place that already works that way than argue for it.
>
> The third is the problem. From what Jane Street engineers have said in public, AI coding agents run across the firm in allow-list sandboxes, and each person has an AI counterpart identity, so what the AI writes and does is attributed to it. That's the most serious approach to agent security I've seen described by a trading firm, and it's the area I want to go deeper in.

Sources for each fact: [block 00](../00-jane-street-intel/README.md). Avoid prestige and pay as reasons; they know, and it reads as the wrong motive.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Why leave J.P. Morgan? You're an SVP.</b> [JS theme]</summary>

> I'm not running away from anything. I own security for a new trading platform and I run the AI security programme in EMEA. But at the bank's scale the job drifts towards coordination, and I'm at my best with my hands on the problem. I'd rather be an engineer on a team of very strong engineers than manage from a distance. Titles at a bank say more about grade than about what you build, and I'm choosing the building.

Never criticise the bank. Answer the question behind it, "will this person actually do hands-on work?", by naming what you build.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>You've applied before. What's changed?</b> [JS: their FAQ says re-interviewing is normal]</summary>

> Yes, I applied before and didn't get through. Since then I've [the one-sentence change from block 25: the public work, the sharper target, the CV rewritten around what I build]. Jane Street's guidance for experienced hires is to wait until something has changed significantly, and I think it has.

One clause about the previous outcome, then what changed. Nothing longer ([block 25](../25-the-application/README.md)).
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>You've moved companies a few times. Why?</b> [practice]</summary>

> Each move was to own more. Synopsys taught me how attackers think. At Wise I grew the security team from five to thirteen. At Lockwood I ran security. At Bell I built security into the delivery pipelines. At J.P. Morgan I own security architecture for a trading platform and run the AI security programme. What I'm looking for now is the place to stay and build.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>What are your salary expectations?</b> [curated]</summary>

At Jane Street, try to let them go first. The firm has a reputation for paying at the top of the market, mostly in cash ([block 13](../13-behavioural/README.md)), so naming a figure first is more likely to cost you than help.

> I'd like to understand the role and the level first. I'm confident that if we get there, we'll land somewhere that works. Could you tell me how the package is structured for this role?

If they press a second time, give a total, not a base:

> Based on what I know of the market for this level, I'd expect total compensation above **£[your ask from the worksheet]**, and I'd weigh how it's split between base and bonus.

Then stop. Don't give a range; its bottom becomes the offer.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>What are you earning now?</b> [curated]</summary>

Nothing stops a UK employer asking, and nothing obliges you to answer. Decide before the call:

- **If your package anchors upwards:** "My base is £[ ], my bonus has averaged [ ]% over two years, and part of it is deferred into J.P. Morgan stock. I'd expect a move to improve on that, and I'd need the deferred part I'd lose to be considered."
- **If it doesn't, or you'd rather not:** "I'd rather not anchor on it, because the roles are different in scope and structure. I'm happy to talk about expectations once I understand the level."
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Is there anything you'd leave behind if you moved?</b> [practice, raise it yourself]</summary>

Only if it's true:

> One thing to flag now so it isn't a surprise later: I have unvested deferred awards at J.P. Morgan that I'd forfeit on resigning. I'd want those considered, as a buyout or in the offer. I can send the schedule when we get there.

Raise it on the first call. A buyout asked for the first time at offer stage looks like a late renegotiation.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>What's your notice period, and when could you start?</b> [curated]</summary>

> My notice period is [N] months. The bank sometimes puts leavers on garden leave for part or all of it, and I won't know until I resign, so the honest answer is a start about [N] months after signing, earlier if I'm released. I'll ask for an early release but I won't promise one.

Know your covenants before the call: notice, non-solicit, any non-compete. A trading firm will ask.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Are you interviewing elsewhere?</b> [curated]</summary>

> Yes, a few conversations, mostly with trading and investment firms, and one is at a late stage. Jane Street is the one I most want, for the reasons I gave. If anything moves elsewhere I'll tell you straight away so we can talk about timing.

Never name the other firms. Never invent an offer or a deadline. A real deadline, with its date, is the most useful thing you can give a recruiter who wants to move you quickly.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>The role is in the office at least three days a week. Is that OK?</b> [JS: the recruiter email for this role said so]</summary>

> Yes, completely. Which days does the team tend to be in?

The recruiter's own email said at least three days a week in London; Blind says most traders are in five and the firm doesn't generally hire fully remote ([block 00](../00-jane-street-intel/README.md)). If you're in five days a week now, say so and move on.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Would you consider the Threat Modelling role as well?</b> [JS: applications are considered across open roles]</summary>

> Yes. Both roles are the same work from two sides: building the controls and modelling what they need to stop. I'd be glad to be considered for either, and I'd like to hear which the team thinks fits better.

Jane Street's FAQ says being considered for more than one team is common ([block 25](../25-the-application/README.md), [block 22](../22-threat-modelling-role/README.md)).
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Do you have the right to work in the UK?</b> [curated]</summary>

Answer exactly as your status stands. If you'd need sponsorship, say so on this call; it's a process question for them, and it only becomes a problem when it surfaces late.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Why should we move you to the next round?</b> [practice, often the recruiter's last question]</summary>

> Because I'm a programmer who does security, which is what the team says it is. I build: Threat-modeling.io is a Python service I wrote and run, and I built a sandboxed multi-agent verifier for security findings. And I work on the problem that's growing fastest at a firm like yours, AI agents with real permissions. I think the team will find a lot to talk about.
</details>

### Your number: the worksheet

| Line | Yours |
| --- | --- |
| A. Current base | £ |
| B. Average bonus over two years, cash and deferred | £ |
| C. Current total (A + B) | £ |
| D. Unvested deferred awards you'd forfeit, at today's value | £ |
| E. **Walk-away, first year** = C × 1.10 + D, unless D is bought out separately | £ |
| F. **The total you'd say if pressed** = the higher of £250k and C × 1.25 | £ |

Why F [inference]: £250k sits below the headline packages for security roles at the top trading firms and well above the general market, so it's an ask that won't price you out and won't leave money on the table if the firm pays as reported. It's an opening ask, not a prediction.

If the offer's base is lower than you hoped, trade in this order: a buyout of D; a guaranteed first-year bonus; the level.

## Questions to ask them

Pick four:

1. What are the next rounds for this role, and is there an online assessment or a live investigation first?
2. Who's on the team, and who would I meet first?
3. How is the package structured at this level?
4. What does the team most want to see in the next round?
5. What's the timeline to a decision?
6. Is there anything in my background you'd like me to expand on before it goes to the team?

The last one is the best closing question on any recruiter call: it brings the doubt out while you can still answer it.

## What gets people cut on this call

- A why-Jane-Street answer about prestige or pay.
- Leading with the title.
- Criticising the current employer.
- Long answers full of product names.
- Inventing an offer or a deadline.
- Promising a start date the notice period can't deliver.
- No questions.
